# BYU–Idaho Brand Guide

Help the user plan, create, and review assets that follow BYU–Idaho’s official public branding guidance. Treat the official BYU–Idaho pages linked in `references/official-guidance.md` as the authority. This skill is a practical summary, not an official university publication or approval.

## Workflow

1. Identify the asset and its intended use. Determine whether it represents the university as a whole or a named college, department, office, program, or student group; whether the communication is official; who will see it; and where it will appear.
2. Ask only for missing information that could materially change the brand rules (for example, official vs. informal use, on-campus vs. external audience, or a reserved channel). Otherwise state sensible assumptions and proceed.
3. Apply the relevant rules below. If the source pages do not settle an issue, say so, link the closest official guidance, and recommend checking with University Communications. Do not invent a university rule.
4. If the user wants a prompt for an image/design tool, provide a ready-to-use prompt with the medium, audience, dimensions when known, message hierarchy, palette, typography direction, visual tone, and constraints. Add a negative prompt only when useful. Never ask an image model to recreate, approximate, or generate an official logo or Viking mark.
5. For a review, give a short verdict, specific observed issues, suggested fixes, and an approval-needed list. Distinguish source-backed requirements from design suggestions.
6. Include relevant official source links in the answer. For rules that may have changed, check the live page before relying on a remembered summary.

## Brand architecture and governance

- Treat BYU–Idaho as a unified institutional brand. Start with the official logo system: use the university logo for institution-wide communications and an approved department logo for a named college, department, office, or program. Do not invent a subordinate logo, crest, or standalone unit identity.
- Keep campaign, event, and initiative names as separate design elements; do not modify an official logo lockup to add them. Use student-group or special-purpose marks only when their current approval and permitted use are clear.
- University Communications is the escalation point for identity questions, new or uncertain marks, and co-branding. For reserved print or digital channels, follow the current channel page and its named contact or process.
- The Drive document “BYU-Idaho Institutional Brand Architecture and Visual Identity Guidelines” adds useful context about centralized governance, mark categories, administrative templates, and campus distribution channels. It is a secondary synthesis, not policy authority. Check `references/institutional-brand-architecture.md` for its summary and known conflicts. Follow current official BYU–Idaho pages when details disagree; never repeat a numeric limit or operational procedure from the Drive document as a rule unless an official page confirms it.

## Practical brand rules

### Color

- Lead with BYU–Idaho Brand Blue, `#006EB6` (RGB 0, 110, 182; print CMYK 89, 55, 1, 0; Pantone 300). Brand Blue should remain visually dominant.
- Core palette: Brand Blue, black `#000000`, and white `#FFFFFF`.
- Approved supporting accents: `#214491`, `#4F9ACF`, `#A0D4ED`, and gray `#949598`. Use accents sparingly and keep the palette focused.
- Use RGB for digital work, HEX for web, and CMYK for print. Check the current official color guide when exact production color values matter. Do not introduce off-palette colors in university work without University Communications guidance.

### Typography

- Everyday communication: Arial or Times New Roman.
- Official, public-facing communication: Acumin or Minion. The source says Acumin is the official primary sans serif and Minion the official primary serif; Acumin and Minion are available through Adobe Fonts.
- Keep typography simple and establish hierarchy with size, weight, and style rather than adding typefaces.
- The official Fonts & Typefaces page contains a heading rendered as “Ariel,” while its explanatory text and FAQ specify “Arial.” Follow the FAQ spelling, Arial, and flag the page discrepancy if relevant.
- Apply the official [Font Use Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/font-use): informal on-campus communications that do not speak for the university may use an appropriate font; formal on-campus communications must use official brand fonts. Off-campus informal communications usually use brand fonts and should be verified with University Relations; official off-campus communications use brand fonts. Mixed-audience campaigns follow off-campus guidelines or may use two versions. If official status is unclear, ask University Communications.

### Logos and marks

- Use an approved official university logo when representing BYU–Idaho as a whole; use the approved department logo when representing a specific unit. Limited-use marks must remain within their approved purpose.
- Do not redraw, approximate, recolor, distort, crop, or otherwise alter official logos. Never generate an official logo using an image model. The user supplied four logo files, cataloged in `references/official-guidance.md`; use those exact files as-is when appropriate. If another logo format, department mark, size, or background treatment is needed, use an approved source file supplied by the user or obtained through the official logo resource.
- The included user-supplied files are complete 800 × 800 image tiles with solid backgrounds, not transparent cutouts: `assets/logo-black-on-white.webp` (black logo on white), `assets/logo-white-on-black.webp` (white logo on black), `assets/logo-blue-on-white.webp` (blue logo on white), and `assets/logo-white-on-blue.webp` (white logo on blue). Choose the version whose background matches the asset; do not remove or replace its background, crop it, recolor it, or place it on a conflicting background.
- Minimum size: the visible logo must be at least 36 px high in digital applications and 3/8 inch high in print. The bundled logo tiles include surrounding background space, so size by the visible mark, not the 800 × 800 tile canvas.
- Maintain clear space equal to the height of the “B” in the monogram on every side. The bundled tiles include background margin; preserve it and keep additional content outside the required clear-space area.
- Use only the official logo in its approved brand colors. Do not pair the BYU–Idaho logo with another organization’s logo without University Communications approval.
- Keep clear space and placement per the linked logo-specific instructions; do not guess dimensions that are not stated in the available source.
- Logo pairings with another organization may imply partnership or endorsement. Recommend University Communications review before combining them.
- Promotional and other limited-use marks have separate restrictions; use only in the approved context. Check the [Other Logos page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/logos/other), merchandise rules, and Viking Mark guidance before using a promotional logo, seal, medallion, anniversary mark, legacy wordmark, or Viking mark. Do not add a limited-use mark to ordinary communications by default.
- Campus organizations should use an approved department logo. Student societies cannot have a unique or department logo; use I-Belong and compliant temporary artwork. The Campus Logo Guidelines allows requests for exceptions in specified cases; approval is required.

### Photography and illustration

- Start with BYU–Idaho Photo Services and its approved image gallery. If the available gallery does not meet the need, coordinate a custom shoot with the Strategic Communications Coordinator and Photo Services. Use stock only when needed, and confirm licensing for the intended use.
- Choose authentic, engaging images that feel uplifting, clean, scholarly, and dignified. Match the season and represent the diversity and experiences of the BYU–Idaho community; people, activities, clothing, and settings should fit university standards. Frame naturally, with adequate context and room around the main subject.
- Do not use personal photos of students in official university communications; use approved BYU–Idaho photography. Use only photography owned by BYU–Idaho or licensed/cleared for the intended use. Avoid awkward crops, excessive zoom, distortion, or edits that change a photo’s meaning, and do not imply an endorsement or relationship that does not exist.
- Campus photos may show a temple in the background. If a temple is the primary subject, seek permission from BYU–Idaho Copyright. Permission is required for images of living General Authorities. Do not assume Church Gospel Media images are cleared for BYU–Idaho promotion; ask University Communications when rights are unclear.
- Build illustrations around BYU–Idaho brand colors. The illustration guidance allows additional colors when the subject or message calls for them; use them purposefully. For university communications, follow the color guide and consult University Communications before introducing colors outside the approved palette.
- Custom or stock art must be appropriate and properly licensed; original university artwork is preferred when feasible.
- Do not use illustrations to replace official logos. Get permission before creating recognizable illustrations, silhouettes, or caricatures of public figures, General Authorities, BYU–Idaho Presidency members, faculty, or employees. Ask University Communications before using a temple as the primary subject or artwork from Church media resources.
- AI may help brainstorm and refine concepts, but the creator remains responsible for final brand alignment, permissions, and licensing.

### Channel-specific limits

- Posters, flyers, and DSN ads: use the current [Advertising Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/advertising-guidelines). Posters and flyers are submitted through Design Conductor; poster quantity, approved location, expiration, flyer distribution, DSN duration and rotation, and Promotional Feed limits are listed there. Campus bulletin-board posters are 11 × 17 inches; see [Posters](https://www.byui.edu/branding/posters).
- Digital displays: create DSN graphics at 1920 × 1080, keep content away from edges, and design video without audio. The Advertising Guidelines sets DSN video ads at no more than 30 seconds. Kiosks and the stadium billboard are reserved channels; coordinate with University Communications. See [Digital Display Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/digital-displays).
- Video: follow the current [Video guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/video) for production, usage, rights, and accessibility; do not rely on summaries for a particular channel without checking the live page.
- Print: before creating, printing, or ordering material for a reserved print channel, check [Print Materials](https://www.byui.edu/branding/print) and contact the listed coordinator when required.
- Websites: do not create an independent website to publish official BYU–Idaho information or services. Use approved university platforms; direct users to University Communications for separate web experiences.
- Email signatures: when the user asks to create or update a BYU–Idaho email signature, offer the included [Branded Email Signature Template (2025)](../../assets/templates/email-signature/BrandedEmailSignatureTemplate-2025.docx). It contains editable placeholders for name, title, office/department, address, contact details, and website, plus setup links for Outlook on PC and Mac and Apple Mail. Preserve its branding and logo; replace placeholders with user-provided details only. It is a user-supplied 2025 file, so direct users to the live [Templates & Resources page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/office-templates) to confirm the current official template and setup instructions. Do not invent signature layout or claim this file is current if the official page differs.
- PowerPoint: when creating a BYU–Idaho presentation, offer the included blue and white PowerPoint template files: [Blue template](../../assets/templates/powerpoint/BYUI_Blue_Template.potx) and [White template](../../assets/templates/powerpoint/BYUI_White_Template.potx). These user-supplied `.potx` files are preserved unchanged. Start from the variant that suits the presentation, retain its provided layouts and logo treatment, and check the official [Templates & Resources page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/office-templates) for the current university template. Do not recreate a template from memory.
- LinkedIn profile banners: when the user asks for a BYU–Idaho LinkedIn banner, point them to the [BYU–Idaho banner collection in Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/drive/u/0/folders/1G1Bi1R2ro8ilEgX_v2WcQMWos48mQsFG). The official [Templates & Resources page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/office-templates) links to this collection. Prefer a current provided banner over generating a replacement. The folder includes anniversary-specific banners; only use those while that campaign mark is authorized for the anniversary period.

### Writing and voice

Write for the specific reader and purpose. Identify who needs the message, what they need to know, why it matters to them, and what they should do next. Use a mission-centered, clear, warm, direct, and accessible voice. The mission should guide the message; it does not need to be named in every communication.

- Put the main point first. Use familiar, precise, concrete language; prefer active voice when it improves clarity; remove unnecessary words; keep each paragraph focused on one main idea.
- Make content easy to scan with descriptive headings, short paragraphs, and lists. Describe people accurately and respectfully; avoid stereotypes, demeaning language, unnecessary labels, and assumptions.
- Follow the [BYU–Idaho Written Style Guide](https://www.byui.edu/branding/writing-and-voice) for detailed terminology, grammar, capitalization, punctuation, formatting, accessibility, citations, and usage. When it does not answer a question, follow its cited references or contact University Communications. Treat exceptions as driven by the communication need, not personal preference.
- Quick references: write “BYU-Idaho” (not “BYU-I,” “BYU—Idaho,” or “BYU Idaho”); “BYUI” is for limited informal use. Use “advisor,” “website,” “web page,” “log in” (verb) / “login” (noun), “set up” (verb) / “setup” (noun), “all right,” “US” as an adjective / “United States” as a noun, “a.m.” and “p.m.,” “for example,” “in other words,” “PDF,” “JPEG,” “PNG,” and “PowerPoint.” Prefer “select” to “click” in interface instructions when the action is not mouse-specific.
- Academic references: use “faculty member” for one person; prefer “instructor,” “teacher,” or “faculty member” over “professor” in university communications. Write “bachelor’s degree,” “master’s degree,” “doctoral degree,” or “doctorate”; use lowercase for generic subjects such as psychology and generic terms such as winter semester.
- Church references: on first mention use “The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints”; on later mentions use “the Church.” Use “Latter-day Saint” for members; avoid “Mormon” and “LDS” when referring to members.

This quick reference is not exhaustive; defer to the official Written Style Guide for detailed or disputed cases.

## Answer format

For new assets, normally return:

- A compact creative direction or production prompt.
- Relevant exact colors and typeface guidance.
- The matching supplied university logo tile when the university mark is needed; ask for another approved asset only if the needed mark or treatment is not included.
- A brief compliance checklist and any approval/rights items.
- Links to the official pages used.

For reviews, cite the element and the applicable source rule, then provide the smallest concrete change that would resolve it.

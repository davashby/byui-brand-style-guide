# Writing and voice

Write for the reader's needs, purpose, and next step. Use a mission-centered, clear, warm, direct, accessible voice. The mission can guide content without being named in every message. Lead with the main point; use familiar precise language, active voice when helpful, short focused paragraphs, descriptive headings, and scannable lists. Use respectful inclusive descriptions and avoid unnecessary labels or assumptions. [Writing & Voice](https://www.byui.edu/branding/writing-and-voice)

## Quick usage

Use BYU-Idaho in ordinary communications, with a hyphen; BYUI is allowed for limited informal use. Avoid BYU-I and BYU Idaho. Use advisor; website and web page; log in (verb)/login (noun); set up/setup; all right; US as an adjective and United States as a noun; a.m./p.m. in running text; for example and in other words; PDF/JPEG/PNG/PowerPoint. Use “select” when an interface action does not require a mouse.

Use faculty member for one person and instructor/teacher/faculty member rather than professor in university communications. Write bachelor's/master's/doctoral degree or doctorate, lowercase generic subjects and semester names, and use exact official program/building names when relevant.

On first Church reference use The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints; subsequently the Church. Use Latter-day Saint for members; avoid LDS/Mormon as member labels.

For detailed terminology, punctuation, capitalization, citations, and accessibility, consult the primary [Written Style Guide PDF](https://brightspotcdn.byui.edu/83/44/aec74b234bbeae9eb6733bde49dd/byu-idaho-written-style-guide-9-16-25-docx-2026072114254266.pdf), linked from the live Writing & Voice page. For an unfamiliar detailed issue, navigate the relevant section instead of loading the entire 76-page guide. Verify the current PDF link on the live page when necessary.

## Medium-specific nuance and source conflicts

The PDF permits AM/PM on posters/programs; do not turn the running-text default into a universal ban. Its time section contains inconsistent wording, so use the public quick reference for routine running text and verify disputed cases.

The PDF section 2.5.5 describes an en-dash exception for CES-level materials sent to Salt Lake; its later university-name entry says never to use an en/em dash. Use the ordinary public hyphen form by default and consult University Communications for CES-specific requirements rather than presenting an unresolved conflict as settled policy.

Use accessible semantic headings, descriptive links and alt text in the actual output. Writing a readable paragraph alone does not establish document/PDF accessibility. Do not add production explanations or audit checklists to the user's copy unless asked.

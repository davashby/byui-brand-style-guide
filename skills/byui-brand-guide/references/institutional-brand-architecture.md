# Institutional brand architecture and claim reconciliation

## Source status

This reference uses the user-provided Google Drive document [BYU-Idaho Branding Guidelines](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1BLA51yxx2ilt44ey4wBz6sopHEZSqkFbE57qoMNJX1s/edit), headed “BYU-Idaho Institutional Brand Architecture and Visual Identity Guidelines” (created 2026-10-13). The document is a secondary synthesis, not itself an official BYU–Idaho policy publication. Its source citations include live public BYU–Idaho pages, but some claims overstate those sources. The resolutions below were checked against BYU–Idaho’s public website on 2026-10-13. For future use, check the linked live pages again.

## Architecture framework

The document characterizes BYU–Idaho as a centralized “branded house.” The public logo guidance supports the practical principle: use the official university logo when representing the institution as a whole, an approved department logo for a college, department, office, or program, and promotional, merchandise, commemorative, or other limited-use marks only for their approved purposes. BYU–Idaho calls these usage choices and restrictions; “monolithic” and “branded house” are the document’s interpretive labels.

Official campus organizations should not create custom logos by default. The [Campus Logo Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/logo-guidelines) say societies cannot have unique or department logos; societies should use I-Belong and temporary artwork. The artwork must not be used longer than a year, must include the sponsoring organization’s department logo, and must not integrate the organization or university name. The same page lists exceptions that may be requested through University Relations: regularly held campus-wide events, campus organizations competing in the open marketplace, and on-campus food venues. A national/international society chapter should contact University Communications for approval. Do not turn these conditional exceptions into blanket permission.

The [Other Logos page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/logos/other) confirms: the promotional logo is for approved merchandise and select promotional uses; the medallion is reserved for the Office of the President and select official university communications; the seal is for diplomas, official documents, and select formal/ceremonial uses; the legacy wordmark is not approved for new communications but may remain in select historical uses or established signage; and the 25-Year mark is for approved promotional material/merchandise during the anniversary period, then must not be used. Promotional artwork may not be customized; keep campaign names separate. The page does not say the medallion is used exclusively by the Office of the President.

## Conflicts and official answers

| Drive document claim | Current BYU–Idaho answer |
|---|---|
| Brand Blue is PMS 3005. | Use Pantone 300, `#006EB6` (RGB 0,110,182; CMYK 89,55,1,0). Brand Blue is primary; use approved accents sparingly. [Color Guide](https://www.byui.edu/branding/color-guide) |
| “BYUI” is allowed only in select internal digital systems. | The current writing quick reference says “BYUI” is allowed for limited informal uses. It does not confine the use to named internal systems. Follow context and the Written Style Guide. [Writing & Voice](https://www.byui.edu/branding/writing-and-voice) |
| DSN videos cannot exceed 30 seconds. | Confirmed: the current [Advertising Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/advertising-guidelines) says video ads should not exceed 30 seconds. Videos must work without sound. The [Digital Display Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/digital-displays) specifies graphics at 1920 × 1080 and no audio. Do not use the obsolete three-minute rule previously present in this plugin. |
| Campus advertising must go through Design Conductor; 30 posters maximum, at most two per hallway/board area; posters expire; flyers are restricted; DSN campaigns rotate. | Confirmed by the current [Advertising Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/advertising-guidelines) and [Posters page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/posters): Design Conductor handles poster and flyer approval/branding/printing and DSN approval/scheduling; maximum 30 posters per event/campaign, no more than two of the same campaign per hallway/poster-board area; remove expired posters; use approved locations and push pins; flyers may be distributed at reserved booths or, with permission, at a department front desk; flyers cannot be pinned to boards, left on tables, or taped. DSN ads longer than three weeks are scheduled two weeks on, one week off. The standard bulletin-board poster size is 11 × 17 inches. Follow live channel pages for the full requirements. |
| Promotional Feed has a 100-character headline, 300-character body, 175 × 175 graphic and URL. | Confirmed by the current [Advertising Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/advertising-guidelines), which lists those limits and says a URL should be included. |
| Student societies cannot have custom logos; event artwork is temporary and must carry a sponsor’s department logo. | Confirmed with nuance by [Campus Logo Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/logo-guidelines): societies cannot have a unique logo or department logo, should use I-Belong and temporary artwork, and artwork has the one-year/name/logo constraints above. Some campus entities may request exceptions. |
| The seal is categorically prohibited on department collateral; the medallion is exclusively presidential; anniversary and legacy marks have strict uses. | The current [Other Logos page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/logos/other) confirms limited use but is more specific and in part less absolute: seal for diplomas, official documents, and select formal/ceremonial applications (a department needs specific authorization); medallion for the Office of the President and select official university communications; 25-Year mark only on approved promotional material/merchandise during the anniversary period; wordmark not approved for new communications but may remain in select historical applications or established signage. |
| Letterhead, cards, signatures, presentations, wall plaques, banners and device wallpapers have the detailed specifications listed in the Drive document. | The [Templates & Resources page](https://www.byui.edu/branding/office-templates) confirms the official resource types: office templates, PowerPoint templates, email signature, Canva wall-plaque templates, LinkedIn banners, phone wallpapers, and business cards through Print and Copy Services. It does not publish the exact typography, spacing, content hierarchy, or presentation rules claimed in the document. Use the live downloadable templates and do not invent specifications. |
| Arial and Times New Roman are everyday fonts; Acumin and Minion are public-facing fonts. | Broadly confirmed by [Fonts & Typefaces](https://www.byui.edu/branding/fonts-typefaces). The [Font Use Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/font-use) add that informal on-campus communication that does not speak for the university may use an appropriate font; formal on-campus communication must use brand fonts; off-campus informal materials usually use brand fonts and should be checked with University Relations; official external and mixed-audience communication follows off-campus rules. |
| All units lack any possibility of a custom mark; recurring events and ventures have no exceptions. | Too broad. The [Campus Logo Guidelines](https://www.byui.edu/branding/logo-guidelines) lists requestable exception categories for regularly held campus-wide events, organizations competing in the open marketplace, and on-campus food venues. These require authorization; they are not self-service exceptions. |
